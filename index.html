About

From lab-scale materials to national energy strategy

I hold an M.S. in Chemical Engineering from UC San Diego (Jacobs School of Engineering, energy concentration, June 2026) and a B.S. in Chemical Engineering from Purdue University — Chemistry minor and Semiconductor Certificate.

My work bridges scales: two years synthesizing chalcogenide perovskite and Cu₂(Ge,Si)S₃ nanomaterials for thin-film photovoltaics in Prof. Rakesh Agrawal's Solar Energy Research Group, a process engineering co-op supporting AES Indiana's $6M coal-to-gas transition, and solar-to-hydrogen pathway modeling that informed a $1M capital decision at Azerbaijan's Ministry of Energy.

Today I focus on electrochemical energy systems, semiconductor processing, and process optimization — from battery characterization (CV, EIS, GITT) to Aspen Plus flowsheets — and I serve as GPSA Departmental Representative for Chemical & Nano Engineering and President of the Azerbaijani Student Association at UCSD.

Experience

Three years, three fronts

Over the past three years I've built experience across the full energy value chain: materials (two years of nanomaterial synthesis and semiconductor-relevant characterization for thin-film photovoltaics), energy systems (a $6M coal-to-gas plant transition at AES Indiana and national-level solar planning at Azerbaijan's Ministry of Energy), and electrochemical storage (battery characterization and grid-scale storage research at UC San Diego). My plan is to keep deepening all three in parallel — taking on progressively more technical ownership in each — to become an engineer who can move fluently between the materials, the cell, and the grid.
